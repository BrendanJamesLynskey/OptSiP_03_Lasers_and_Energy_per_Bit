Why Silicon Needs Someone Else's Laser

Silicon has an indirect bandgap: the lowest state of its conduction band and the highest state of its valence band sit at different crystal momenta. An electron and a hole can only recombine and emit a photon with the help of a phonon to carry the momentum difference, so radiative recombination is slow and loses out to non-radiative paths. Silicon makes poor light emitters and no practical electrically pumped laser. III–V semiconductors (InP, GaAs and their alloys) have direct gaps and make the lasers of every optical link.

The consequence

  • A silicon photonic chip is a modulator-and-detector chip: the light comes from a III–V device, attached, bonded, grown or fibred in (slide 02).
  • The laser is often the least reliable and most temperature-sensitive part of the link, which pushes it away from hot electronics (slide 03).

The opportunity

  • Keeping the laser separate lets one source feed many modulators, or many wavelengths (slide 05).
  • Every dB lost between laser and detector is paid in laser power at the wall, divided by the laser's efficiency: the thread through the rest of this deck.

Reviews: Liang and Bowers, “Recent progress in lasers on silicon” (Nature Photon. 4, 511, 2010); Komljenovic et al., “Heterogeneous silicon photonic integrated circuits” (J. Lightwave Technol. 34(1), 20, 2016).

03

External Lasers and the ELSFP

  • Why move the laser out. In co-packaged optics the optical engines sit beside a high-power switch ASIC; lasers dislike heat, and are the part most likely to fail. A front-panel laser module keeps them cool and lets a failed one be swapped without opening the package.
  • The OIF External Laser Small Form Factor Pluggable (ELSFP) implementation agreement, OIF-ELSFP-01.0 (August 2023), specifies such a module: a front-panel, hot-swappable laser source with a blind-mate multi-fibre optical connector at the rear for eye safety, and management over CMIS; a later revision, 02.0, followed (OIF implementation agreements).
  • The co-packaged module it feeds: OIF's 3.2T co-packaged module agreement specifies 8 × 400G (FR4 or DR4) optics behind a 32-lane CEI-112G-XSR host interface (OIF-Co-Packaging-3.2T-Module-01.0).
  • The price is an extra fibre-to-chip coupling for the laser light, and polarisation-maintaining fibre: decibels that appear in the budget of slide 08. The data-centre series takes co-packaged optics further.
04

Wall-Plug Efficiency

Wall-plug efficiency (WPE) is optical power out over electrical power in. It converts every optical decibel in a link into electrical watts: the laser's electrical power is Poptical/WPE, and each 3 dB of extra loss doubles it.

FigureWhat it isSource
Up to 13%Measured WPE of a short-cavity III–V-on-silicon C-band DFB laser (200 µm) at 20 °C, coupling up to 2 × 6 mW into the silicon waveguideRahimi Vaskasi et al. (Proc. SPIE 12006, 120060H, 2022)
35% × 41% = 14.35%A DFB pump's WPE times a Kerr comb's conversion efficiency, assumed for a comb-driven transmitterRizzo et al. (arXiv:2109.10297)
  • Laser makers advertise higher values for high-power CW DFB chips intended as co-packaged-optics sources; treat these as the vendors' claims until measured in a system.
  • WPE falls with temperature, and drive and cooling electronics sit outside it: a thermo-electric cooler can cost more than the laser. The interactive on slide 09 lets you set WPE and see the laser's share of the energy per bit move.
05

Multi-Wavelength Sources

A WDM link needs one carrier per wavelength. Three ways to provide them:

Laser arrays

One DFB per wavelength, each held on its grid slot by its own temperature or current. Simple and powerful, but the count of lasers, and of things that fail, grows with the channel count.

Mode-locked comb lasers

One laser emits many equally spaced lines. Liu et al.'s quantum-dot mode-locked laser grown on silicon gave 58 lines within 3 dB at 20 GHz spacing and carried 4.1 Tbit/s as 64 channels of 32 GBd PAM4, with an external optical amplifier (Optica 6, 128, 2019).

Kerr microcombs

A CW pump in a high-Q microresonator generates a comb by four-wave mixing. Rizzo et al. drove a silicon photonic link with one: 32 wavelengths at 16 Gb/s each, 512 Gb/s, with bit error ratios below 10−12 (Nature Photon. 17, 781, 2023).

  • The catch is per-line power. A comb shares its output between its lines, and the outer lines are weaker. The budget is set by the weakest line, not the average, so comb flatness becomes a loss term.
  • Ring-based WDM transmitters (deck 02) pair naturally with combs: one bus, one comb, one ring per line.
06

Fibre Attach and Packaging

  • Alignment is the cost. A single-mode fibre has to sit on a coupler to a fraction of a micrometre, and stay there through curing, temperature cycles and years. Arrays of fibres in a V-groove block are aligned together, often actively (with light on) and then fixed with adhesive.
  • The coupler decides the package: grating couplers take fibres from above, anywhere on the die, and allow wafer-level test; edge couplers need the fibre at a prepared facet but give lower loss and broader bandwidth (deck 01, slide 06).
  • Polarisation and lasers add their own requirements: polarisation-maintaining fibre for the laser input, isolation against reflections, and a thermal path that keeps rings and lasers stable.
  • Carroll et al. describe photonic packaging as orders of magnitude more expensive than electronic packaging and perhaps the most significant bottleneck to silicon photonics in volume (Appl. Sci. 6, 426, 2016).

  • Why distance costs energy. Flip-chip and 3-D bonds keep these femtofarads small.
  • Monolithic removes the interface entirely but takes the photonics in a transistor-optimised process, or the transistors in a photonics-optimised one; 3-D lets each chip use its own best process.
08

The Optical Link Budget

laser power− laser-to-chip coupling− modulator loss− mux− chip-to-fibre− fibre + connectors− fibre-to-chip− demux≥ sensitivity + margin
  • Work backwards from the receiver. Its sensitivity is an OMA (deck 02, slide 12). With extinction ratio r = P1/P0, the average power that delivers a given OMA is Pavg = OMA·(r + 1)/(2(r − 1)): a modest extinction ratio needs well over the OMA in average power.
  • Add every loss in dB to get the laser power; divide by the wall-plug efficiency to get electrical power. Fibre is the smallest term in a data centre: SMF-28 Ultra is specified at no more than 0.32 dB/km at 1310 nm and 0.18 dB/km at 1550 nm (Corning PI-1424).
  • PAM4 raises the required OMA by about 4.8 dB at the same noise (deck 02, slide 01); a shared laser split N ways costs 10·log10N dB per branch.
  • Margin covers ageing, temperature and manufacturing spread; standards write their budgets the same way, from transmitter and receiver specifications plus a channel allocation.

Thermal Tuning in the Budget

Rings must be held on their wavelengths (deck 02, slide 08), and heaters draw power whether or not data flows. Per bit, a heater costs its power divided by the lane's data rate.

Heater power per ringAt 10 Gb/sAt 25 Gb/sAt 50 Gb/s
0.5 mW50 fJ/bit20 fJ/bit10 fJ/bit
2 mW200 fJ/bit80 fJ/bit40 fJ/bit
5 mW500 fJ/bit200 fJ/bit100 fJ/bit

Arithmetic: mW ÷ Gb/s = pJ/bit. Heater powers are illustrative; measured heaters need 12.7–24.77 mW for a full π (deck 02, slide 08), and a ring needs a fraction of that depending on its offset.

  • Tuning can exceed modulation by orders of magnitude. A 0.9 fJ/bit ring modulator (Timurdogan et al.) beside a 2 mW heater at 25 Gb/s spends nearly a hundred times more on staying tuned than on modulating.
  • Rizzo et al.'s design estimates for a comb-driven ring transmitter make the same point: about 173 fJ/bit for ring tuning against 12.5 fJ/bit for modulation, with about 180 fJ/bit for the receiver (arXiv:2109.10297; estimates, not measurements).
  • Remedies: faster lanes (the same heater over more bits), wider FSRs so offsets are smaller, better process control, athermal designs, thermal isolation that raises heater efficiency, and sharing heat between neighbouring rings.

What to Take Away

  • Silicon borrows its laser. External, attached, heterogeneous or monolithic: the choice is about heat, reliability and replaceability as much as coupling loss. Co-packaged optics leans towards external front-panel sources (OIF ELSFP).
  • Every decibel is paid at the wall, divided by a wall-plug efficiency of about 13% in the measurement and 14% in the comb analysis cited here; vendors claim higher.
  • Packaging, aligning fibres to sub-micrometre tolerance and keeping them there, is a large share of the cost.
  • Energy per bit is laser, driver, receiver, clocking and tuning: modulators reach femtojoules, whole links tens of femtojoules to picojoules. Always ask what a figure includes (slide 10; the interactive, slide 09).
  • Tuning can dominate a ring-based link; faster lanes and better process control are the main levers.
